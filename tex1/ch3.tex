\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:prelim}

\subsection{Linear Input-Varying (LIV) Operators}

The LIV framework \cite{thomas2024star,massaroli2024liv} provides a unified algebraic view of sequence mixing operators.
Its central insight is to replace the \emph{fixed} weight matrix of a standard linear layer with a weight that is \emph{assembled from the input itself}.

In a LIV block, four learned projections of the input $x$ produce feature vectors $B$, $C$, $S$, $V$:
\begin{equation}
  B = W_B x, \quad C = W_C x, \quad S = f_S(x), \quad V = W_V x,
  \label{eq:featurizer}
\end{equation}
where $W_B, W_C, W_V$ are linear layers and $f_S$ is a linear or small MLP map.
The $L \times L$ token-mixing matrix $T$ is then assembled algebraically: $B$ and $C$ determine gate strength via dot-product, $S$ encodes the structural pattern (decay, kernel, or identity), and $V$ carries the payload — never entering $T$ itself.

Concretely, each entry of $T$ is:
\begin{equation}
  T_{ij} = \langle\mathbf{c}_i,\, \mathbf{s}_{ij} \odot \mathbf{b}_j\rangle,
  \label{eq:tij}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{s}_{ij} \in \mathbb{R}^d$ is the \emph{structural modulator} from $S$: the all-ones vector for LOW\_RANK, the cumulative product $\prod_{k=j}^{i-1}\mathbf{a}_k$ for SEMI\_SEPARABLE, and a scalar $K_{i-j}$ for TOEPLITZ.
The output is:
\begin{align}
  \mathrm{mixed}_i &= \sum_j T_{ij} \cdot \mathbf{v}_j,
  \label{eq:token_mix} \\
  Y_i &= W \cdot \mathrm{mixed}_i,
  \label{eq:channel_mix}
\end{align}
where $W$ is a static learned parameter independent of $x$.

\subsubsection{Token Mixing Families}

Four choices of structural modulator $\mathbf{s}_{ij}$ span the space of practical sequence operators.

\textbf{LOW\_RANK} ($\mathbf{s}_{ij} = \mathbf{1}/\sqrt{d}$):
\begin{equation}
  T = CB^\top / \sqrt{d}.
\end{equation}
Every pair $(i,j)$ interacts; row-wise softmax recovers multi-head attention \cite{vaswani2017attention}.
Cost $\mathcal{O}(L^2 d)$; full bidirectional context.

\textbf{SEMI\_SEPARABLE} ($\mathbf{s}_{ij} = \prod_{k=j}^{i-1}\mathbf{a}_k$, $\mathbf{a}_k \in (0,1)^d$ from $S$):
\begin{equation}
  T_{ij} = \left\langle\mathbf{c}_i,\; \left(\prod_{k=j}^{i-1}\mathbf{a}_k\right) \odot \mathbf{b}_j\right\rangle \;\; (j \le i), \quad T_{ij} = 0 \;\; (j > i).
\end{equation}
The cumulative product decays older inputs exponentially, computed via \texttt{torch.cumprod}. Recovers SSMs and CfC.

\textbf{TOEPLITZ} ($\mathbf{s}_{ij} = K_{i-j}\,\mathbf{1}$, shift-invariant scalar kernel from $S$):
\begin{equation}
  T_{ij} = \langle\mathbf{c}_i,\, \mathbf{b}_j\rangle \cdot K_{i-j}, \quad K_{i-j} = 0 \;\text{if}\; |i-j| \ge k.
\end{equation}
$K$ is a learned parameter (fixed kernel) or produced by a small MLP (input-dependent variant \cite{poli2023hyena}).

\textbf{DIAGONAL} ($\mathbf{s}_{ij} = \delta_{ij}$):
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{mixed}_i = \langle\mathbf{c}_i,\, \mathbf{b}_i\rangle\;\mathbf{v}_i.
\end{equation}
With $\mathbf{b}_i = \mathrm{SiLU}(W_{\mathrm{gate}}\,x_i)$ this recovers SwiGLU FFN \cite{shazeer2020glu} at $\mathcal{O}(Ld)$ with no cross-token interaction.

The four families trade off context range against cost — DIAGONAL $<$ TOEPLITZ $<$ SEMI\_SEPARABLE $\approx$ LOW\_RANK in context width — while SEMI\_SEPARABLE and DIAGONAL share $\mathcal{O}(Ld)$ complexity versus $\mathcal{O}(L^2d)$ for LOW\_RANK.

\subsection{CfC Networks and their LIV Representation}
\label{sec:cfc_liv}

We use CfC (Closed-form Continuous-time) networks \cite{hasani2021cfc} as the SEMI\_SEPARABLE operator in the LIV-TS pool.
Their complementary gating constraint ($g_t + (1{-}g_t)=1$) enforces a convex interpolation between retained state and new input at every step, preventing gradient vanishing — a stability property Mamba's unconstrained transition $A_k$ does not guarantee.
Derived from Liquid Time-Constant neurons \cite{hasani2022liquid}, the CfC state update is:
\begin{equation}
  h_t = g_t \odot h_{t-1} + (1 - g_t) \odot \tilde{h}_t,
  \quad g_t = \sigma(W_g\,x_t + b_g),
  \label{eq:cfc_recurrence}
\end{equation}
where $g_t \in (0,1)^d$ is the forget gate and $\tilde{h}_t = \tanh(W_{\mathrm{in}}\,x_t)$ is the candidate state.
Unrolling this recurrence shows that $h_i$ is a weighted sum of all past inputs with the cumulative product $\prod_{k} g_k$ as the structural modulator $\mathbf{s}_{ij}$ — exactly the SEMI\_SEPARABLE pattern — confirming that CfC can be searched interchangeably with attention and convolution operators within the LIV genome.

\subsection{NSGA-II}

NSGA-II \cite{deb2002nsga2} is a fast elitist multi-objective evolutionary algorithm.
Candidates are ranked by Pareto dominance — genome $a$ dominates $b$ if it is no worse on both objectives and strictly better on at least one — and ties are broken by crowding distance, which favours individuals in sparse regions of objective space.
At each generation, tournament selection picks parents based on (rank, crowding distance); offspring are produced by multi-point crossover and per-gene mutation at rate $p_{\mathrm{mut}} = 0.10$.
Elitism guarantees monotone improvement: the next population is drawn from the merged parent-offspring pool sorted by Pareto rank, so the front never degrades.

\subsection{TiDAR: Think-in-Diffusion Autoregressive Prediction}

Standard autoregressive (AR) forecasting generates the horizon sequentially at $\mathcal{O}(H)$ forward passes.
TiDAR \cite{tidar2024} proposes a hybrid training objective that enables parallel \emph{draft} prediction of all $H$ steps in a single pass, with optional AR refinement.

The $L+H$ tokens are processed jointly under a hybrid sparsity mask: lookback tokens attend causally among themselves; forecast positions are masked but attend bidirectionally to the full lookback.
The training loss combines a next-step AR objective on the lookback with a parallel prediction objective on the masked horizon:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{TiDAR}} = \frac{1}{1+\alpha}\bigl(\alpha \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{AR}} + \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Diff}}\bigr).
  \label{eq:tidar_loss}
\end{equation}
At inference, three operating modes trade quality against speed: pure draft ($\mathcal{O}(1)$), partial-AR ($\mathcal{O}(k)$), and full-AR ($\mathcal{O}(H)$).

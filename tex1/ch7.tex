\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented \textbf{LIV-TS}, a framework that reformulates multivariate time series architecture design as a multi-objective evolutionary search over Linear Input-Varying (LIV) operators.
By encoding forecasting architectures as genomes of interchangeable LIV operator classes and running NSGA-II to jointly minimise validation MSE and parameter count, LIV-TS consistently discovers heterogeneous operator mixtures that dominate every homogeneous baseline on the accuracy-compactness Pareto frontier.
Across all three benchmarks (ETTh1, ETTm1, Exchange-Rate) and both structural templates (S-Mamba, iTransformer), no single-class configuration matches the trade-off achieved by a mixed genome, confirming that operator-family selection is a consequential yet underexplored design dimension.
We additionally adapt the TiDAR training scheme to continuous-valued time series regression, achieving an 80.8$\times$ wall-clock speedup over full autoregressive decoding with negligible accuracy loss — an improvement that arises directly from training-inference consistency rather than model size reduction.
Cross-dataset transfer experiments further show that genomes discovered on ETTh1 generalise to ETTh2 and Exchange-Rate without re-running evolution, outperforming the all-Mamba baseline by up to 63.6\%, demonstrating that evolutionary search recovers meaningful structural inductive biases rather than dataset-specific artefacts.
These results collectively establish that operator choice should be treated as a first-class search variable in time series model design, and that the LIV algebra provides a principled grammar for doing so efficiently.
